Churn Model: Summary

What we did

We tested the current Taxonomy churn score on real churned customers from Anand's HEKA data. We scored each one 3 months before it churned and compared it with the live fleet as "retained" customers. Then we built better models on the same data.

The current model

Score = 25·Traffic falling off + 25·Shrinking deployment + 8·Going quiet + 8·Gone dark. A customer is flagged at 35 or more.

In practice, a customer is only flagged when traffic is falling and members are being removed at the same time.

Results

Current model Best new model (gradient boosting)
Churners caught 1 of 378 163 of 378
Flags that were real churners 4% 42%
Churners found in the top 10% riskiest grids 7% 43%
ROC-AUC (0.5 = guessing, 1.0 = perfect) 0.52 0.84

The current model shows 90% "accuracy", but that number is misleading. Only about 10% of customers churn, so flagging nobody also scores about 90%.

What we learned

  1. Customers don't wind down before they leave. Members, objects and traffic barely change in the year before churn, and about half are still reporting after the contract ends. Churn is a business decision, not a technical shutdown.
  2. Going silent is the best warning sign. About a third of churners stop reporting for 21+ days in the months before churn, rising to 41% by the churn date. Among retained customers, 12% trip the "Gone dark" signal.
  3. Who the customer is matters most. Churners are thin deployments:
  4. no DNS Traffic Control;
  5. low reporting use;
  6. about 3,000 daily queries vs 1.5 million for retained customers;
  7. fewer records, admins and dashboards.

Infoblox isn't deeply embedded for them, so it's easy to replace. 4. Two current signals work backwards. "Traffic falling off" and "Going quiet" fire more often on customers who stayed.

What to look at next

Using HEKA data we already have: 1. Signs of moving away from Infoblox: DNS zones showing up in AWS or Azure, more forwarding or secondary zones, or Microsoft DNS taking over. 2. Paid features used vs paid for: customers paying for features they don't use. 3. Licence events: temporary licences, licences about to expire, licences being dropped. 4. Old hardware or no upgrades for a long time: the hardware-refresh decision is a natural moment to leave. 5. Reporting patterns: intermittent reporting or opt-outs.

Needing other data: 6. Salesforce / Gainsight: days to renewal, falling ARR, support cases, NPS. 7. Moves to Infoblox cloud: some "churners" may have moved to BloxOne / Universal DDI, which isn't a real loss. 8. Admin logins and API use: true engagement, not collected today.

Test first: #1 and #7, which are possible today with data we already have.

Caveats

  • The "retained" group is a stand-in. It may include some churners, which would make the new model look worse than it really is.
  • Ask Anand for the same data on customers who renewed, with account IDs. Rerunning python -m churn.run then gives the final accuracy.
